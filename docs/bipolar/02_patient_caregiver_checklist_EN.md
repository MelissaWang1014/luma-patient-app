# Bipolar Disorder — Patient and Caregiver Monitoring Checklist

**Educational material derived from published guidelines. It does not replace the treating clinician's instructions. Where this document and your prescriber disagree, follow your prescriber.**

Sources: Nierenberg AA et al., *JAMA*. 2023;330(14):1370-1380 · VA/DoD Clinical Practice Guideline for Management of Bipolar Disorder v2.0 (2023) · CANMAT/ISBD 2018 guidelines · ISBD/IGSLI Task Force on lithium levels (Nolen WA et al., *Bipolar Disord*. 2019;21(5):394-409) · Cochrane review of early-recurrence recognition (Gupta S et al., 2025).

**How to read the numbers.** Thresholds marked **[G]** come directly from a guideline or drug label. Thresholds marked **[P]** are practical trigger points a care team commonly agrees on with a family — they are reasonable starting values, but you should confirm them with your own clinician and write your personalized numbers in the blank column.

## Section 1 — Daily Tracking Log

Track these six items every day. Monitoring is only useful when it is paired with an agreed action plan; tracking without a plan can increase anxiety and, in depression, may prolong the episode.

| # | Item | How to record | Personalized threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mood rating | −5 (worst depression) to +5 (highest elevation), 0 = baseline | ______ |
| 2 | Total sleep time | Hours slept, and whether you *felt tired* the next day | ______ |
| 3 | Medication taken | Yes / No / partial, for each drug and each dose time | ______ |
| 4 | Daily rhythm | Wake time, first social contact, bedtime | ______ |
| 5 | Energy and activity | Number of new projects started; unusual spending | ______ |
| 6 | Stress, alcohol, cannabis, caffeine | Amounts, not just yes/no | ______ |

## Section 2 — Episode Duration Thresholds (Diagnostic Reference)

These are the durations clinicians use to define an episode. They are **not** the point at which you should call — call earlier, at the early-warning stage in Section 3.

| Pattern | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Manic episode** | Elevated, expansive, or irritable mood **plus** increased energy/activity, most of the day nearly every day, **≥1 week** (or any duration if hospitalization is required), with **≥3 additional symptoms** (≥4 if the mood is only irritable) | **[G]** DSM-5-TR |
| **Hypomanic episode** | Same symptom set, **≥4 consecutive days**, observable change in functioning but no marked impairment, hospitalization, or psychosis | **[G]** DSM-5-TR |
| **Major depressive episode** | **≥5** depressive symptoms for **≥2 weeks** | **[G]** DSM-5-TR |
| **Cyclothymic disorder** | **≥2 years** (1 year in youth) of subthreshold symptoms present at least half the time | **[G]** DSM-5-TR |
| **Rapid cycling** | **≥4 mood episodes of any polarity within 12 months** — associated with greater morbidity, higher suicide-attempt rates, and poorer mood-stabilizer response | **[G]** DSM-5 specifier |

> The common phrase "two weeks of mania" mixes two different thresholds. **Mania is ≥1 week. Depression is ≥2 weeks.** The two-week number that matters for mania is the **prodrome window**: most people show early warning signs **2 to 4 weeks before** a full episode.

## Section 3 — Early Warning Signs (The 2-to-4-Week Window)

Early warning signs are individual but consistent for the same person across episodes. Family members often notice them before the patient does. Fill in the personal list with your clinician after each episode.

### 3.1 Signs of an approaching manic or hypomanic episode

The single most prominent manic prodrome is sleep disturbance, reported by approximately **77%** of patients.

| Sign | Practical trigger to act |
|---|---|
| Decreased **need** for sleep (sleeping less but not tired) | Sleep drops **≥2 hours below personal baseline for ≥3 consecutive nights** with no drop in energy **[P]** |
| Increased goal-directed activity | **≥2 new major projects or commitments started in one week** **[P]** |
| Increased self-esteem, grandiosity | Any statement about special abilities or a special mission **[P]** |
| Heightened brightness of colors, loudness of sounds | Any new report of this **[P]** |
| More talkative, racing thoughts | Others repeatedly say they cannot follow or interrupt **[P]** |
| Overspending | Any unplanned purchase **above an amount agreed in advance** with the family — e.g. USD 500 **[P]** |
| Increased sexual interest, risk-taking | Any behavior outside the person's established pattern **[P]** |

### 3.2 Signs of an approaching depressive episode

| Sign | Practical trigger to act |
|---|---|
| Loss of interest and pleasure | ≥3 consecutive days of withdrawing from usually enjoyed activities **[P]** |
| Hypersomnia | Sleep **≥2 hours above baseline for ≥3 consecutive nights** **[P]** |
| Fatigue, slowed movement or speech | Noticed by two different people **[P]** |
| Concentration failure | Unable to complete routine work tasks for ≥3 days **[P]** |

### 3.3 The agreed action plan (write this in advance, not during an episode)

1. Protect sleep first: fixed bedtime, no screens after a set hour, no naps after mid-afternoon.
2. Reduce stimulation: fewer commitments, fewer late events, less travel.
3. Hold caffeine, alcohol, and cannabis at zero or at the usual stable level — do not change abruptly if on lithium (see Section 5).
4. Verify every medication dose was taken for the last 7 days.
5. Contact the treatment team. Do not wait for the full episode to declare itself.
6. Enact the pre-agreed financial safeguard (e.g., a spending limit or a second signature).

## Section 4 — Urgent and Emergency Thresholds

**Contact the treatment team the same day if any of these appear:**

- Any psychotic symptom: fixed false beliefs, hearing or seeing things others do not
- **Mixed features** — high energy or agitation occurring together with hopelessness or despair. This combination carries an especially elevated suicide risk and always warrants same-day contact.
- Any new thoughts of death, hopelessness, or of not wanting to be alive
- Signs of lithium toxicity (Section 5.2)
- Not sleeping at all for **2 consecutive nights** **[P]**
- Stopping medication for **≥3 days** **[P]**

**Go to emergency services immediately if:** there is intent or a plan to harm oneself or another person, or the person cannot be kept safe at home.

- **US crisis line: call or text 988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), available 24/7.
- Means safety — restricting access to anything the person could use to harm themselves — should be planned in advance **with the treatment team**, not improvised during a crisis.

## Section 5 — Medication Checklist

### 5.1 Daily medication rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Take doses at the **same clock time daily** | Timing consistency affects both serum levels and social rhythm stability |
| Lithium is usually consolidated to **once daily at bedtime** in maintenance | **[G]** VA/DoD Appendix E |
| Never stop abruptly | Abrupt lithium discontinuation is associated with rebound mania |
| Never adjust the dose yourself | Even a "small" self-adjustment can move lithium out of range — the therapeutic index is narrow (~2) **[G]** |
| Report side effects rather than stopping | Roughly **60%** of patients with bipolar disorder are non-adherent at some point, and non-adherence is a leading cause of relapse **[G]** |
| Antidepressants alone are not treatment | Antidepressant monotherapy is not recommended; it can trigger mania and more frequent switching **[G]** |

### 5.2 Lithium — the numbers that matter

**Blood draw rule:** always a **trough** level, drawn **11–14 hours after the last dose** (commonly stated as ~12 hours). A level drawn at the wrong time is uninterpretable. **[G]**

| Situation | Target serum level | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Acute mania | **0.8–1.2 mEq/L** | **[G]** FDA label; CANMAT/ISBD 2018 |
| Maintenance, standard | **0.6–0.8 mmol/L** | **[G]** ISBD/IGSLI Task Force |
| Maintenance, good response but poor tolerance | may reduce to **0.4–0.6 mmol/L** | **[G]** ISBD/IGSLI |
| Maintenance, insufficient response, good tolerance | may increase to **0.8–1.0 mmol/L** | **[G]** ISBD/IGSLI |
| Below **0.4 mmol/L** | **Ineffective** | **[G]** ISBD/IGSLI |
| Above **1.0 mmol/L** in maintenance | **No added benefit**; VA/DoD notes higher risk of eventual kidney harm and advises avoiding if possible | **[G]** |
| Age 65–79 | usually **0.4–0.6**, maximum 0.7–0.8 mmol/L | **[G]** ISBD/IGSLI |
| Age >80 | usually **0.4–0.6**, maximum 0.7 mmol/L | **[G]** ISBD/IGSLI |

**Warning levels — call the prescriber or seek urgent care:**

| Serum level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **>1.2 mEq/L** outside an acute manic episode | Above target — contact prescriber |
| **1.5–2.0 mEq/L** | Mild toxicity range |
| **2.0–2.5 mEq/L** | Moderate toxicity — urgent medical evaluation |
| **>2.5 mEq/L** | Severe toxicity — emergency |

**Symptoms of lithium toxicity — do not wait for a blood test:** coarse hand tremor (different from the fine tremor that is a common side effect), vomiting, diarrhea, unsteady walking, slurred speech, muffled thinking, unusual drowsiness or confusion. Any of these means **call immediately**.

**When to check a level:** approximately **5 days after any dose change** (3 days per the FDA label), then every **3–6 months** once stable. Two consecutive therapeutic levels are needed before considering the acute target achieved. **[G]**

### 5.3 Other medications — what to watch

| Drug | Key number or warning |
|---|---|
| **Valproate / divalproex** | Trough **50–125 mcg/mL** for acute mania. Teratogenicity approximately **6%** — a **strong recommendation against** use in anyone of childbearing potential **[G]** |
| **Carbamazepine** | Teratogenicity **3–6%**; also strongly recommended against in childbearing potential **[G]** |
| **Lamotrigine** | Requires a mandatory **6-week slow titration**. Rash occurs in **5–10%**; Stevens-Johnson syndrome in about **0.02%**. **Any new rash = stop and call the same day.** Maximum 400 mg/day **[G]** |
| **Atypical antipsychotics** | Watch weight. Gain of **≥7% of body weight** occurred in 30.3% on olanzapine+fluoxetine, 28.4% olanzapine, 5.4% lurasidone, 3.9% quetiapine, 3.1% cariprazine, 0.8% lumateperone, versus 1.1% on placebo **[G]** |

### 5.4 Monitoring schedule to keep track of

| Test | Schedule |
|---|---|
| Lithium level, BUN/creatinine | Every **3–6 months** **[G]** |
| TSH, calcium, weight (on lithium) | At **6 months**, then annually **[G]** |
| CBC, liver function, electrolytes, weight (on valproate/carbamazepine) | Every **3 months in year 1**, then annually **[G]** |
| Body mass index (on antipsychotics) | Baseline, **1, 2, 3, and 6 months**, then annually **[G]** |
| HbA1c, fasting glucose, fasting lipids (on antipsychotics) | Baseline, **3 months**, then annually **[G]** |
| Blood pressure | Every 3 months in year 1, then annually **[G]** |

## Section 6 — Sleep Quality Targets

| Item | Target | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Total sleep time | **7–9 hours** for most adults **[P]** | Consistency matters more than the exact number |
| Bedtime and wake time | Within **±30 minutes** of the same clock time every day, including weekends **[P]** | A weekday-to-weekend shift is a documented relapse trigger |
| Time to fall asleep | Under 30 minutes **[P]** | |
| Naps | Avoid after mid-afternoon; keep under 30 minutes **[P]** | |
| Caffeine cutoff | **8 hours** before bedtime **[P]** | On lithium, keep the *total daily amount* constant (Section 7) |
| Screens | Off **1 hour** before bed **[P]** | |
| Red flag | Sleep **≥2 hours below** baseline for **≥3 nights** with preserved energy **[P]** | Treat as a pre-manic signal, not as insomnia |

## Section 7 — Diet

There is no established bipolar-specific therapeutic diet. The consensus recommendation is a **Mediterranean-style, anti-inflammatory pattern**, which also addresses the elevated cardiometabolic risk seen in bipolar disorder. The strongest, most actionable dietary rules are the **lithium interactions**, which are safety-critical.

### 7.1 Lithium–diet rules (safety-critical)

Lithium is handled by the kidney like sodium, so diet directly shifts serum levels.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Keep daily salt intake consistent** | A sudden **decrease** in sodium increases renal lithium reabsorption and can push levels toward toxicity. A sudden **increase** lowers levels and reduces efficacy. |
| **Keep fluid intake steady; avoid dehydration** | Fever, vomiting, diarrhea, heavy sweating, intense exercise, and hot weather concentrate lithium and can precipitate toxicity. Roughly **2–2.5 L/day** is a common starting target **[P]** — confirm with your clinician. |
| **Keep caffeine intake stable** | Caffeine increases lithium excretion. Cutting back abruptly raises levels and worsens tremor; increasing it lowers levels. Pick a fixed number of cups per day and hold it. |
| **Discuss crash diets, very-low-salt, and low-protein diets before starting them** | All shift lithium levels. Check a level after any major dietary change. |
| **Avoid grapefruit juice** | Flagged in the VA/DoD guideline as a food interaction in bipolar pharmacotherapy; it also affects several antipsychotics metabolized by CYP3A4 (quetiapine, lurasidone, cariprazine, lumateperone). |
| **Lurasidone must be taken with food** | Requires a meal of at least **350 calories** for adequate absorption **[G]** |

### 7.2 Foods to emphasize

- Vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts, olive oil
- Seafood, particularly oily fish — omega-3 intake is the most-studied nutrient and is associated with improvement in the **depressive** pole (not mania)
- High-fiber carbohydrates, folate, and zinc sources

### 7.3 Foods to limit

- Refined carbohydrates and added sugar, trans and low-quality fats, heavily processed foods
- **Nitrated cured and processed meats** — associated with mania in human data and mania-like hyperactivity in animal models
- Alcohol and non-prescribed substances
- Any abrupt change in total salt, fluid, or caffeine (see 7.1)

### 7.4 One-day sample menu (Mediterranean pattern, lithium-compatible)

The point of this menu is **regularity** — three meals at roughly the same times each day, with stable salt, fluid, and caffeine. Portion sizes should be set with a dietitian or clinician; no calorie target is given here.

**Breakfast (07:30)**
- Rolled oats cooked with milk or unsweetened soy milk
- Mixed berries and a small handful of walnuts
- Plain Greek yogurt
- **One cup of coffee — the same amount every day**
- One glass of water

**Mid-morning snack (10:30)**
- An apple or pear with a small handful of unsalted almonds
- Water

**Lunch (12:30)**
- Grilled salmon or sardines
- Quinoa or a whole-grain roll
- Large leafy green salad with tomato, cucumber, and red onion, dressed with extra-virgin olive oil and lemon
- Water
- *If taking lurasidone at midday, this meal satisfies the ≥350-calorie requirement.*

**Afternoon snack (15:30)**
- Hummus with carrot and bell pepper sticks
- Water
- No caffeine after this point if bedtime is 22:30

**Dinner (18:30)**
- Lentil and vegetable stew (onion, carrot, celery, tomato, spinach) with olive oil
- Small portion of brown rice or whole-grain bread
- Side of steamed broccoli
- Water

**Evening (21:00)**
- Small plain yogurt or a piece of fruit if hungry
- Take bedtime medication at the **same time every night**
- Water; taper fluids close to bedtime only if nocturia is disrupting sleep — discuss with the prescriber rather than restricting fluids on lithium

**Daily totals to hold steady:** salt (do not swing between very salty and salt-free days), fluid (~2–2.5 L unless otherwise instructed), caffeine (fixed number of cups).

## Section 8 — Caregiver-Specific Checklist

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **You are a primary data source** | Manic and hypomanic symptoms are often far more obvious to family than to the patient, whose insight may be absent during an episode. Your observations are clinically valuable — bring them to appointments. |
| **Ask about the reason for irritability** | In mania, irritability is usually provoked by others obstructing what the person wants to do or buy. In PTSD, it is provoked by trauma reminders. This distinction helps the clinician. |
| **Watch for concrete behavioral markers** | New insomnia pattern, increased substance use, multiple job or housing changes, infidelity in an established relationship, unanticipated legal or financial problems — these often represent emerging mania. |
| **Agree on financial safeguards in advance** | Set a spending threshold and a second-signature rule while the person is well, not during an episode. |
| **Do not argue with grandiose or delusional content** | Redirect to safety and to contacting the treatment team. |
| **Family-focused therapy and psychoeducation work** | They reduce relapse rates, hospitalization, and improve medication adherence. Ask the team for a referral. |
| **Look after yourself** | Caregiver support programs are a guideline recommendation (VA/DoD Recommendation 41), not an optional extra. |
| **Keep the crisis plan visible** | Team phone number, after-hours number, 988, nearest emergency department, current medication list. |

## Section 9 — Appointment Preparation Sheet

Bring to every visit:

- [ ] Daily tracking log since the last visit
- [ ] Current medication list with actual doses taken (not prescribed doses)
- [ ] Any missed doses and the reason
- [ ] Side effects, with dates of onset
- [ ] Weight since last visit
- [ ] Recent lab results, with the **time of the last dose before each lithium draw**
- [ ] Any change in salt, fluid, caffeine, alcohol, or cannabis intake
- [ ] Any new medication from another prescriber, including over-the-counter drugs and supplements — NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, and thiazide diuretics all raise lithium levels
- [ ] Caregiver's observations, written down in advance

*This checklist covers monitoring and self-management only. Diagnosis, medication selection, and dose changes belong to the treating clinician. If you or someone you care for is in crisis in the US, call or text 988.*
