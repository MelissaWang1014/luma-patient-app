## 4. Clinical Narrative

### 4.1 Chief complaint

Ten days of elevated mood, markedly reduced sleep, and disorganized behavior.

### 4.2 History of present illness

The patient developed elevated and irritable mood approximately 10 days prior to presentation, with no identifiable precipitant. She has slept only 2 to 3 hours per night since onset yet reports feeling energized rather than fatigued — a decreased *need* for sleep rather than insomnia with daytime fatigue. Speech became noticeably rapid with frequent topic shifts. During this period she placed online orders for approximately USD 8,000 of photography equipment and stated her intention to launch a studio within three months, despite no prior interest or experience in photography.

Five days before admission she began voicing grandiose beliefs, stating that she had been "chosen to transform the industry." Her spouse reports she is unable to remain seated, repeatedly abandons and restarts plans, and has stopped eating regular meals. She has shown verbal impulsivity toward family members but no physical aggression and no expressed intent to harm herself or others. Cannabis use has increased over the past two weeks.

Symptom duration exceeds the DSM-5-TR threshold of ≥1 week for a manic episode, with ≥3 additional symptom criteria met (grandiosity, decreased need for sleep, pressured speech, flight of ideas, distractibility, increased goal-directed activity, and excessive involvement in activities with a high potential for painful consequences).

### 4.3 Past psychiatric history

First depressive episode at age 22, diagnosed as major depressive disorder. Sertraline was initiated; within three weeks she experienced a period of markedly increased energy with approximately five consecutive nights of minimal sleep. This episode was not recognized as antidepressant-associated hypomania at the time and no diagnostic revision was made.

Four subsequent depressive episodes followed, two of which were treatment-resistant. The interval between her first depressive episode and the present recognition of bipolar disorder is **12 years**, consistent with the reported mean diagnostic delay of approximately 9 years after a first depressive episode.

Retrospective features that should have raised suspicion for bipolar disorder, per VA/DoD Sidebar 1:
- Mood elevation and irritability emerging after antidepressant initiation
- Extended periods of high functioning on little sleep
- Onset of depression at a young age (≤25 years)
- Multiple prior depressive episodes (≥5)
- Treatment-resistant depression
- Family history of cyclical mood disorder and completed suicide in a first-degree/second-degree relative

### 4.4 Current medications on admission

| Medication | Dose | Duration | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sertraline | 100 mg PO daily | 6 months | To be tapered and discontinued (VA/DoD Sidebar 7) |

No other prescription medications. No over-the-counter supplements reported. No St. John's Wort. No stimulant-containing workout supplements.

### 4.5 Family history

Mother with a described history of severe mood fluctuation, never formally diagnosed. Maternal aunt died by suicide. Family history is a recognized supporting feature for bipolar disorder when psychotic or manic symptoms are absent at presentation.

### 4.6 Social history

Married, one child. Employed as a market analyst; currently suspended. Smokes 15 cigarettes per day for 12 years. Alcohol 2 to 3 occasions per week. Cannabis use increased over the past 2 weeks. **No current contraception in use** — documented because it constrains mood-stabilizer selection. Housing stable. Spouse present and engaged as primary collateral informant and support person.

### 4.7 Mental status examination

- **Appearance and behavior:** Brightly and unusually dressed. Restless; pacing throughout the room, unable to remain seated.
- **Speech:** Increased in rate and volume, pressured; interruptible but rapidly returns to prior stream.
- **Mood and affect:** Elevated, with intermittent irritability. Affect labile and congruent with elevated mood.
- **Thought process:** Flight of ideas; loosening of associations.
- **Thought content:** Mood-congruent grandiose delusions. No suicidal or homicidal ideation. No obsessions.

- **Cognition:** Alert, fully oriented to person, place, time, and situation. Attention markedly impaired by distractibility.
- **Insight:** Absent — denies being ill.
- **Judgment:** Impaired — evidenced by large impulsive purchases and unrealistic business plans.

### 5.1 Differential diagnosis considered and excluded

| Alternative | Reason excluded |
|---|---|
| Substance-induced mood disorder | Symptoms preceded the escalation in cannabis use and are disproportionate to cannabis effects; no stimulant or hallucinogen on urine drug screen |
| Primary psychotic disorder | Manic symptoms (increased energy, decreased need for sleep, goal-directed activity) preceded psychotic content; delusions are mood-congruent |
| Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder | Symptoms are episodic and represent a clear break from previous functioning, not a lifelong non-episodic pattern |
| Borderline personality disorder | Mood symptoms have a discrete onset and are not reactive to interpersonal abandonment; no identity disturbance |
| Anxiety disorder | Reduced sleep is a decreased *need* for sleep with preserved energy, not initial or middle insomnia with daytime fatigue |
| Thyrotoxicosis | TSH normal |

## 9. Notes for Model Training Use

- Every value in this record is synthetic. If used as a seed for augmentation, vary demographics, labs, and episode features independently; do not treat any single value here as an empirical distribution.
- The record deliberately encodes several teachable patterns: diagnostic delay after a first depressive episode, antidepressant-associated hypomania that was missed, mood-congruent psychosis, and mood-stabilizer selection constrained by childbearing potential.
- Real de-identified longitudinal bipolar data is available through the NIMH Data Archive (STEP-BD cohort, approximately 4,300 participants with medication, scale, and outcome data) under a data-use agreement. MIMIC-IV contains complete ICU orders and time-series but few psychiatric admissions.
