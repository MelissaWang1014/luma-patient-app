# Bipolar Disorder Pharmacotherapy — Professional Reference Summary

**Scope.** A consolidated, guideline-referenced summary of pharmacological treatment for bipolar I and bipolar II disorder in adults, organized by illness phase and stratified by severity. Intended as a study and reference document for trainees. Not a prescribing authority: all dosing must be verified against the current FDA label, and agent selection must account for the individual patient's comorbidities, prior response, tolerability, and preferences.

**Primary sources.** Nierenberg AA, Agustini B, Köhler-Forsberg O, et al. *JAMA*. 2023;330(14):1370-1380 · VA/DoD Clinical Practice Guideline for Management of Bipolar Disorder, Version 2.0 (2023) · Yatham LN et al. CANMAT/ISBD 2018, *Bipolar Disord*. 2018;20(2):97-170 · Nolen WA et al. ISBD/IGSLI Task Force, *Bipolar Disord*. 2019;21(5):394-409 · Yildiz A et al. *Lancet Psychiatry*. 2023;10(9):693-705 (acute depression NMA) · Kishi T et al. *Mol Psychiatry*. 2022;27(2):1136-1144 (acute mania NMA) · Nestsiarovich A et al. *Eur Neuropsychopharmacol*. 2022;54:75-89 (maintenance).

**Notation.** VA/DoD recommendation numbers appear as **[R#]** with GRADE strength. Only three recommendations in the 2023 VA/DoD guideline are *strong for* — R12, R18, R23 — and one is *strong against* — R30.

## 1. Drug Classes

| Class | Agents used in bipolar disorder | Principal role |
|---|---|---|
| **Lithium salts** | Lithium carbonate, lithium citrate | Only agent with established anti-suicide effect; prevents both poles |
| **Anticonvulsant mood stabilizers** | Valproate/divalproex, lamotrigine, carbamazepine | Valproate — antimanic; lamotrigine — antidepressant-pole prophylaxis; carbamazepine — second line |
| **Second-generation antipsychotics (SGA)** | Quetiapine, olanzapine, aripiprazole, asenapine, cariprazine, lurasidone, lumateperone, paliperidone, risperidone, ziprasidone | Phase-dependent; the only class with agents FDA-approved for acute bipolar depression |
| **First-generation antipsychotics (FGA)** | Haloperidol | Acute mania and acute agitation only |
| **Long-acting injectables (LAI)** | Aripiprazole monohydrate, risperidone LAI, paliperidone palmitate | Maintenance in poor adherence or inadequate oral response |
| **Benzodiazepines** | Lorazepam and others | Acute agitation adjunct only; not for maintenance |
| **Antidepressants** | SSRIs, bupropion, venlafaxine, fluoxetine (in OFC) | Never monotherapy; limited adjunctive role, mainly bipolar II |

## 2. Acute Mania and Hypomania

Treatment principles are identical for mania and hypomania; the difference is setting and urgency. Before initiating: taper and discontinue antidepressants and stimulants, address medical contributors, address intoxication and withdrawal, and avoid valproate/carbamazepine/topiramate if the patient is of childbearing potential (VA/DoD Sidebar 7).

### 2.1 Stratified by severity

#### Tier 1 — Mild to moderate mania or hypomania, outpatient, no psychosis

**Monotherapy.**

| Line | Agents | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| First | **Lithium** or **quetiapine** | **[R5]** weak for |
| Second | **Olanzapine**, **paliperidone**, or **risperidone** | **[R6]** weak for |
| Third | **Aripiprazole**, **asenapine**, **carbamazepine**, **cariprazine**, **haloperidol**, **valproate**, or **ziprasidone** | **[R7]** weak for |

Odds ratios for response versus placebo in acute mania (network meta-analysis): risperidone 1.69, haloperidol 1.64, olanzapine 1.59, cariprazine 1.56, quetiapine 1.55, aripiprazole 1.53, lithium 1.45, valproate 1.42, ziprasidone 1.35, paliperidone 1.34, asenapine 1.28. Carbamazepine 1.90 — highest point estimate but a less favorable tolerability and interaction profile.

#### Tier 2 — Moderate to severe mania, breakthrough on monotherapy, or inadequate response

**Combination therapy: mood stabilizer + antipsychotic.**

| Combination | Reference |
|---|---|
| **Lithium or valproate** + **haloperidol, asenapine, quetiapine, olanzapine, or risperidone** | **[R8]** weak for |

Selection between combination partners should be driven by prior treatment response, severity, and tolerability. Combination therapy is frequently necessary in severe mania.

#### Tier 3 — Severe mania with psychotic features, marked agitation, or absent insight

- Combination therapy as in Tier 2, generally inpatient.
- **Benzodiazepines** and **haloperidol** for acute agitation management — short-term only, not recommended for long-term use.
- **ECT** in combination with pharmacotherapy when rapid symptom control is required in bipolar I with acute severe manic symptoms — **[R31]** weak for.
- Inpatient care is often required for safety; **involuntary admission is appropriate when impaired judgment adversely affects the capacity to consent**. Patients who are aggressive or psychotic are more likely to require involuntary admission.

### 2.2 Explicitly not recommended in acute mania

| Recommendation | Content |
|---|---|
| **[R9]** weak against | Brexpiprazole, topiramate, or lamotrigine as monotherapy for acute mania |
| **[R10]** weak against | Adding aripiprazole, paliperidone, or ziprasidone after unsatisfactory response to lithium or valproate monotherapy |
| **[R11]** insufficient evidence | Other FGAs/SGAs, gabapentin, oxcarbazepine, or benzodiazepines as monotherapy or in combination for acute mania |

## 3. Acute Bipolar Depression

Five agents or combinations are FDA-approved for acute bipolar depression: **olanzapine/fluoxetine combination (OFC), quetiapine, lurasidone, cariprazine, lumateperone**.

### 3.1 Stratified by severity

#### Tier 1 — Mild to moderate bipolar depression

| Line | Agents | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| First | **Quetiapine monotherapy** | **[R12]** — **STRONG FOR**, the only strong recommendation for an acute-phase agent |
| Second | **Cariprazine, lumateperone, lurasidone, or olanzapine** monotherapy | **[R13]** weak for |

#### Tier 2 — Moderate to severe, or inadequate monotherapy response

| Combination | Reference |
|---|---|
| **Lamotrigine + lithium** or **lamotrigine + quetiapine** | **[R15]** weak for |
| **Olanzapine + fluoxetine (OFC)** | Highest response rate in the network meta-analysis; highest metabolic cost |

Adjunctive lamotrigine trial data: 51.6% versus 31.7% response when added to lithium over 8 weeks (n = 124); 31% versus 16% when added to quetiapine over 12 weeks (n = 202).

#### Tier 3 — Severe, psychotic, treatment-refractory, or requiring rapid response

- **ECT** — the most effective treatment for severe and treatment-resistant depression including bipolar depression. Meta-analysis of 567 patients: **77.1% response, 52.3% remission**. Swedish register study (n = 1,251): 80.2% much or very much improved on CGI-I, though observational design precludes causal inference. Adverse effects usually transient: headache 48%, short-term memory loss ~41%, nausea 23%, muscle pain 15%. Indicated additionally for suicidal ideation, severe illness, pregnancy, and poor medication tolerability in older patients.
- **Light therapy** as short-term augmentation to pharmacotherapy in BD I or BD II — **[R32]** weak for.
- **rTMS** as adjunctive treatment after partial or no response to pharmacotherapy for depressive symptoms — **[R33]** weak for.

### 3.2 Comparative efficacy — acute bipolar depression

Network meta-analysis of 101 RCTs, 20,081 participants, 2–16 weeks. Response = 50% reduction in depressive score. Placebo response rate 34.7%.

| Agent | Response rate | Statistically superior to placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Olanzapine + fluoxetine | 56.7% | Yes |
| Quetiapine | 49.0% | Yes |
| Lurasidone | 48.5% | Yes |
| Cariprazine | 44.5% | Yes |
| Lamotrigine | 44.4% | Yes |
| Olanzapine | 44.3% | Yes |
| Lumateperone | 42.4% | Yes |
| Paroxetine | 42.1% | No |
| Lithium | 38.1% | No |
| Aripiprazole | 35.2% | No |

Weight gain ≥7% of body weight over the same trials: OFC 30.3%, olanzapine 28.4%, lurasidone 5.4%, quetiapine 3.9%, cariprazine 3.1%, lumateperone 0.8%, lamotrigine 0.4%, placebo 1.1%. **Efficacy ranking and metabolic-risk ranking are almost inverted** — this trade-off is the central decision in acute bipolar depression.

### 3.3 Lamotrigine — the discordance between guidelines

Lamotrigine is not FDA-approved for acute bipolar depression, and VA/DoD **[R14]** finds insufficient evidence for lamotrigine monotherapy in the acute phase. CANMAT/ISBD nonetheless recommends it first-line for acute depression in bipolar I and second-line in bipolar II. The reconciliation: individual-patient-data meta-analysis of 5 trials (n = 1,072, 7–10 weeks) showed 46.6% versus 38% response (RR 1.22, 95% CI 1.06–1.41, NNT = 13), but the mandatory 6-week dose titration required to mitigate rash risk blunts measurable effect in short trials. Its favorable tolerability and depressive-episode prophylaxis are what earn it a first-line position in some guidelines.

### 3.4 Antidepressants in bipolar depression

| Point | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Monotherapy is not recommended**, particularly in bipolar I | Risk of induced mania/hypomania and increased switching |
| **Contraindicated** in the presence of manic symptoms or mixed features | Agitation or irritability may signal mixed features; antidepressants should be discontinued |
| **Adjunctive use** has limited evidence | Two meta-analyses: mean difference in depressive score change −0.13 (95% CI −0.24 to −0.02) — statistically significant, not clinically meaningful. No significant difference in response rates (~48% vs 43%) |
| **Longer-term adjunctive use raises switch risk** | Over a 52-week extension: mania/hypomania 17% versus 10%, OR 1.77 (95% CI 1.02–3.09) |
| **Maintenance adjunctive antidepressants did not prevent relapse** | RCT, n = 209, bipolar I in remission, escitalopram or bupropion + mood stabilizer/antipsychotic for 52 weeks: 31% vs 46%, HR 0.68 (95% CI 0.43–1.10), not significant |
| **VA/DoD position** | **[R14]** and **[R17]** — insufficient evidence for antidepressants as monotherapy or as augmentation |
| **Where they may still be considered** | Bipolar II, always paired with a mood stabilizer or antipsychotic; evidence base for BD II specifically is thin |
| **Ketamine/esketamine** | **[R16]** insufficient evidence as monotherapy or adjunct |

## 4. Maintenance and Relapse Prevention

Most patients require lifelong treatment. Agents effective in the acute phase are typically continued into maintenance.

### 4.1 Prevention of manic recurrence

| Line | Agents | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| First | **Lithium** or **quetiapine** | **[R18]** — **STRONG FOR** |
| Second | Oral **olanzapine**, oral **paliperidone**, or **risperidone LAI** | **[R19]** weak for |
| Combination | **Aripiprazole, olanzapine, quetiapine, or ziprasidone** + **lithium or valproate** | **[R22]** weak for |
| Against | **Lamotrigine monotherapy** for mania prophylaxis | **[R21]** weak against |
| Insufficient | Other FGAs/SGAs and anticonvulsants including valproate | **[R20]** |

### 4.2 Prevention of depressive recurrence

| Line | Agents | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| First | **Lamotrigine** | **[R23]** — **STRONG FOR** |
| Also first | **Lithium** or **quetiapine** monotherapy | **[R24]** weak for |
| Second | **Olanzapine** monotherapy | **[R25]** weak for |
| Combination | **Olanzapine, lurasidone, or quetiapine** + **lithium or valproate** | **[R26]** weak for |
| Insufficient | Other FGAs/SGAs and anticonvulsants, alone **[R27]** or in combination **[R28]** | |

### 4.3 Maintenance efficacy (odds ratios for prevention of relapse to any mood episode)

| Agent | Absolute relapse rate vs comparator | OR |
|---|---|---|
| Asenapine | 8.7% vs 33.3% | 5.26 |
| Olanzapine | 23.9% vs 56.4% | 3.85 |
| Quetiapine | 21.5% vs 50% | 2.78 |
| Aripiprazole | 26.5% vs 51% | 2.63 |
| Risperidone LAI | 39% vs 56.4% | 2.44 |
| Lithium | 24.5% vs 50.5% | 2.17 |
| Valproate | 24% vs 39% | 2.04 |
| Lamotrigine | 38.4% vs 46.6% | 1.52 |

### 4.4 Predominant-polarity matching

Agent choice should track the patient's predominant polarity:

| Predominant polarity | Preferred agents |
|---|---|
| Manic | Aripiprazole, risperidone LAI, paliperidone, olanzapine — stronger antimanic prophylaxis |
| Depressive | Lamotrigine, quetiapine, lurasidone — stronger antidepressive prophylaxis |
| Balanced | Lithium, quetiapine, olanzapine — cover both poles |

## 5. Common Treatment Combinations — Consolidated Table

| Clinical scenario | Combination | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Severe mania, monotherapy failure | Lithium **or** valproate **+** haloperidol / asenapine / quetiapine / olanzapine / risperidone | **[R8]** |
| Acute mania with severe agitation | Above **+** short-course benzodiazepine (lorazepam) | JAMA review; short-term only |
| Acute bipolar depression, inadequate monotherapy | Lamotrigine **+** lithium, **or** lamotrigine **+** quetiapine | **[R15]** |
| Acute bipolar depression, severe | Olanzapine **+** fluoxetine (OFC) | FDA-approved; highest efficacy, highest metabolic cost |
| Mania prophylaxis, monotherapy insufficient | Aripiprazole / olanzapine / quetiapine / ziprasidone **+** lithium or valproate | **[R22]** |
| Depression prophylaxis, monotherapy insufficient | Olanzapine / lurasidone / quetiapine **+** lithium or valproate | **[R26]** |
| Bipolar II with residual depression | Mood stabilizer or SGA **+** antidepressant (never antidepressant alone) | JAMA review; weakest evidence base |
| Poor oral adherence | LAI (aripiprazole monohydrate, risperidone LAI, paliperidone palmitate) **±** oral mood stabilizer | **[R19]**; JAMA review |
| Bipolar I with refractory severe mania | Pharmacotherapy **+** ECT | **[R31]** |
| Refractory bipolar depression | Pharmacotherapy **+** ECT; or **+** rTMS; or **+** light therapy | **[R31]–[R33]** |
| Any phase, non-acute-manic | Pharmacotherapy **+** psychotherapy (CBT, family/conjoint, IPSRT, psychoeducation ≥6 sessions) | **[R34]** |

**Polypharmacy caution.** Regimens combining a mood stabilizer, an antipsychotic, and an antidepressant are common but increase non-adherence, which already affects roughly **60%** of patients. Simplify schedules where possible and provide psychoeducation addressing the drivers of non-adherence.

### 7.2 Mood stabilizer adverse effects

| Agent | Adverse effects with rates where reported |
|---|---|
| **Lithium** | Tremor 14%, hypothyroidism 14%, sedation 10%, weight gain 3%, chronic kidney disease 1%, hyperparathyroidism |
| **Valproate** | Alopecia, sedation, weight gain, tremor, transaminase elevation, teratogenicity ~6% |
| **Carbamazepine** | Sedation, nausea, teratogenicity 3–6%, hyponatremia, blood dyscrasias |
| **Lamotrigine** | Rash 5–10%, Stevens-Johnson syndrome 0.02%, minimal weight effect, teratogenicity ~2–3% |

### 7.3 Lithium long-term organ effects

| Domain | Data |
|---|---|
| **Thyroid** | Hypothyroidism 13.9% vs 1.7% over a mean of 70.1 months (OR 5.78, 95% CI 2.00–16.67). Readily treated with levothyroxine; little evidence that stopping lithium restores thyroid function |
| **Parathyroid** | PTH increased by 7.32 pg/L (95% CI 3.42–11.23); calcium increased by 0.09 mmol/L. Primary hyperparathyroidism 10% in lithium users versus 0.1% in the general population |
| **Renal** | GFR decline 30% greater than aging alone in patients treated a mean of 18 years. Decline 0.92% per treatment year plus 0.71% per age year, versus 0.64% age-related decline in the general population. **6–10 years** of exposure required before significant decline; **30 years** before 18.1% reach stage 3 CKD. Stage 4 incidence low (0.0004/patient-year) |
| **Risk factors for CKD** | Longer treatment duration, higher serum lithium levels, older age, medical comorbidity |
| **Mitigation** | Target the lower end of the therapeutic range; VA/DoD advises avoiding levels >1.0 mEq/L in maintenance |

### 7.4 Lithium's protective associations

Compared with other medication classes in bipolar disorder:

| Outcome | Rates | Effect size |
|---|---|---|
| Suicide | 0.4% vs 1.8% | OR 0.26 (95% CI 0.09–0.77) |
| All-cause mortality | 1.3% vs 2.8% | OR 0.42 (0.21–0.87) |
| Dementia | 3.04% vs 6.85% | OR 0.51 (0.36–0.72) |
| Osteoporosis | 5.98 vs 9.07 per 1,000 person-years | HR 0.62 (0.53–0.72) |
| Cancer | IRR 0.94 (0.72–1.22) vs 1.24 (1.05–1.46) | — |

### 7.5 Antipsychotic mortality signal

Five-year longitudinal study, 49,293 individuals with bipolar disorder. Antipsychotic exposure showed a **dose-related increase in all-cause mortality** compared with unexposed patients with bipolar disorder:

| Exposure | Definition | HR |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Half the recommended maintenance daily dose | 1.13 |
| Moderate | >0.5 to <1.5 times the daily dose | 1.69 |
| High | >1.5 times the recommended daily dose | 2.08 |

Cardiovascular mortality showed the same dose-related pattern, highest in the high-exposure group (HR 2.08, 95% CI 1.69–2.57). This argues for using the lowest effective antipsychotic dose and for reassessing the need for continued antipsychotic exposure in maintenance.

## 8. Therapeutic Drug Monitoring and Laboratory Schedule

### 8.1 Lithium serum targets by phase

| Phase / population | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Acute mania | 0.8–1.2 mEq/L (WFSBP permits up to 1.3; Cochrane suggests 0.6–0.8 per NICE/BAP) | FDA label; CANMAT/ISBD |
| Maintenance, standard | 0.60–0.80 mmol/L | ISBD/IGSLI Task Force |
| Maintenance, reduce for tolerability | 0.40–0.60 mmol/L | ISBD/IGSLI |
| Maintenance, increase for response | 0.80–1.00 mmol/L | ISBD/IGSLI |
| Ineffective | <0.40 mmol/L | ISBD/IGSLI |
| No added maintenance benefit | >1.00 mmol/L | ISBD/IGSLI |
| Age 65–79 | 0.40–0.60, max 0.70–0.80 mmol/L | ISBD/IGSLI |
| Age >80 | 0.40–0.60, max 0.70 mmol/L | ISBD/IGSLI |

Sampling: trough, 11–14 hours post-dose (~12 h). Recheck approximately 5 days after any dose change (3 days per FDA label), then every 3–6 months once stable. Establish two consecutive therapeutic levels during the acute phase before considering the target achieved. Therapeutic index approximately 2.

### 8.2 Monitoring schedules

| Drug | Parameter | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Lithium** | Serum level, urea, creatinine | Every 3–6 months |
| **Lithium** | TSH, calcium, weight | At 6 months, then annually |
| **Valproate** | Serum level | As clinically indicated |
| **Carbamazepine** | Serum level | Approximately every 6 months |
| **Valproate / carbamazepine** | Weight, CBC, LFTs, electrolytes, urea, creatinine | Every 3 months in year 1, then annually |
| **Valproate / carbamazepine** | Inquiry re: menstrual changes | Every 3 months in year 1 |
| **Lamotrigine** | Clinical monitoring for rash | Ongoing; no routine level |
| **Antipsychotics** | Body mass index | Baseline, 1, 2, 3, 6 months, then annually |
| **Antipsychotics** | HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose, fasting lipid panel | Baseline, 3 months, then annually |
| **Antipsychotics** | Blood pressure | Every 3 months in year 1, then annually |

### 8.3 Interactions that shift lithium levels

Raise levels: NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, thiazide diuretics, dehydration, sodium restriction, abrupt caffeine reduction.
Lower levels: increased sodium intake, increased caffeine, osmotic diuretics, theophylline, pregnancy (increased GFR).

## 9. Special Populations

### 9.1 Childbearing potential and pregnancy

| Recommendation | Content |
|---|---|
| **[R30] STRONG AGAINST** | **Valproate, carbamazepine, and topiramate** in the treatment of bipolar disorder in individuals of childbearing potential. Teratogenicity: valproate ~6%, carbamazepine 3–6%. Also associated with neurodevelopmental delay and reduced IQ in offspring |
| **[R29]** weak for | For those stabilized on lithium who are or may become pregnant, continue lithium at the **lowest effective dose**, within a framework of psychoeducation and shared decision-making |
| Practical | Anyone of childbearing potential using valproate or carbamazepine must receive explicit counseling on these risks and a contraceptive strategy. Lamotrigine may be safer (malformation risk estimated 2–3%) but data are limited |

### 9.2 Older adults

Lower lithium targets (Section 8.1). Initiate antipsychotics, carbamazepine, valproate, and cariprazine at the lower end of the dosing range. Lithium doses are usually lower due to age-related GFR reduction.

### 9.3 Renal and hepatic impairment

| Agent | Renal | Hepatic |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium | Reduce for CrCl 30–<60; **avoid** CrCl <30 | No adjustment |
| Cariprazine | Not recommended CrCl <30 | Not recommended Child-Pugh C |
| Lurasidone | 20 mg once daily if CrCl <50 | 20 mg start, 80 mg cap in Child-Pugh B/C |
| Asenapine | No adjustment | **Contraindicated** Child-Pugh C |
| Divalproex | No adjustment | Not recommended Child-Pugh A/B; **contraindicated** Child-Pugh C |
| Lumateperone | No adjustment | Avoid Child-Pugh B/C |
| Lamotrigine | No adjustment | Reduce 25% (Child-Pugh B/C without ascites); reduce 50% (with ascites) |

### 9.4 Comorbid conditions

| Comorbidity | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Tobacco use disorder** | Varenicline for cessation, with monitoring for increased depression and suicidal behavior — **[R43]** weak for. Smoking prevalence in bipolar disorder ~45%, approximately 3.5-fold the general population |
| **Substance use disorder** | Insufficient evidence for any specific pharmacotherapy or psychotherapy — **[R44]**; refer to the VA/DoD SUD guideline |
| **Residual anxiety in remitted BD** | CBT — **[R45]** weak for |
| **Cardiometabolic risk** | Collaborative care with systematic monitoring of cardiovascular risk factors, smoking cessation support, physical activity and diet quality promotion. Prevalence in bipolar disorder: metabolic syndrome 37%, obesity 21%, type 2 diabetes 14%. Life expectancy reduced 12–14 years; cardiovascular SMR 1.73 |

## 11. Practical Decision Sequence

1. **Establish the phase.** Mania/hypomania, depression, mixed features, or euthymia. The phase determines the drug, not the diagnosis alone.
2. **Screen for childbearing potential.** This single question removes valproate, carbamazepine, and topiramate from the list before anything else is considered.
3. **Check for mixed features.** If present, antidepressants are contraindicated and suicide risk assessment takes priority.
4. **Discontinue destabilizers.** Taper antidepressants and stimulants in mania; address substances.
5. **Establish predominant polarity** from the longitudinal history — this determines the maintenance agent.
6. **Baseline labs before initiation.** TSH, CBC, comprehensive metabolic panel, urine drug screen, pregnancy test, ECG if a QTc-prolonging agent is planned; add HbA1c and lipids before any antipsychotic.
7. **Select the agent** by phase and severity per Sections 2–4, weighting metabolic burden (Section 7.1) against efficacy (Sections 2.1, 3.2).
8. **Set the monitoring schedule at the time of prescribing**, not later (Section 8.2).
9. **Plan maintenance from the start.** Agents effective acutely are usually continued; most patients need indefinite treatment.
10. **Address adherence explicitly.** Simplify the regimen, provide psychoeducation, and consider an LAI where oral adherence is poor.
